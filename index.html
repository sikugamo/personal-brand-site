    I design software for people whose work is mostly thinking: researchers, editors, analysts, and the teams that support them. My aim is always the same. The tool should get quieter the longer you use it.

    I started in agency work, shipping marketing sites on deadlines that left no room to ask whether anyone needed them. Then I spent four years as design lead at a small note-taking company, where I learned how much a product's defaults shape the way people think. Since 2023 I've worked independently with two or three teams a year.

    Alongside client work, I write essays about the craft and publish the templates I actually use. If something here helped you, I'd like to hear about it.

    How I work

    1. i.Start with the tenth session.

      First impressions matter less than the hundredth use. I design for the person who already knows the product.

    2. ii.Write before drawing.

      Every project begins with a one-page brief. If it can't be written plainly, it isn't ready to be designed.

    3. iii.Small teams, long engagements.

      I work embedded for three to six months, alongside the engineers who'll maintain the result.
